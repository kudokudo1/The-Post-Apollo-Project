# CONTRIBUTING // META APOLLO // POST-APOLLO FAMILY

**Contributions to the Meta Apollo // Post-Apollo Family are welcome!**

Community is obviously a very important part of that.

Meta Apollo is built around relationships: relationships between people and tools, people and machines, ideas and implementations, projects and their histories, and people with one another.

**Community is one of the most meaningful ways those relationships grow.**

A project does not only grow because its original author adds more code. It grows when other people use it, question it, break it, repair it, reinterpret it, criticize it, teach with it, build on it, and bring back something the original project did not have before.

Those exchanges create new relationships.

That is something we want.

A contribution can be code, but it does not have to be. Bug reports, documentation, testing, experiments, design criticism, evidence, questions, research, artwork, examples, architectural observations, and even a careful explanation of why something feels wrong can all contribute to the project.

The goal is not to make every contributor think the same way.

The goal is to allow the project to change through relationships without losing the ability to understand what changed, where it came from, and why.

**That means contributions should preserve continuity, provenance, and spirit.**

Continuity means the project can change without losing track of what it was.

Provenance means we can still tell where ideas, code, decisions, and artifacts came from.

Spirit means preserving the recognizable intent and relationship that made the project itself, even when its implementation changes.

Before making a significant change, spend enough time with the repository to understand what you are changing and what relationship that part of the system was designed to serve.

Read the README, architecture notes, design material, and any project-specific documentation that applies.

You do not have to preserve every implementation decision that came before you. Post-Apollo is supposed to evolve.

But if you change something important, make the change legible.

If you replace a project-specific mechanism with a generic one, explain why.

If you change a relationship between components, users, tools, services, or environments, explain what changed.

If something is being removed, explain what is being lost as well as what is being gained.

Keep contributions focused enough that another person can reasonably understand what happened.

Preserve existing attribution, provenance, license notices, and third-party history.

Do not submit code, writing, artwork, data, or other material that you do not have the right to contribute.

## YOUR WORK REMAINS YOUR WORK

If you create something and contribute it to the Meta Apollo // Post-Apollo Family, you keep your copyright.

You are not giving up ownership of your work.

But by intentionally submitting copyrightable material for inclusion in a project, you give the project permission to actually use that contribution and continue carrying it forward.

That means you grant the repository owner and maintainers a permanent, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free right to use, reproduce, modify, distribute, display, sublicense, and relicense your contribution as part of the project.

That permission also allows your contribution to be distributed under the project's community licensing terms and to be included in separately negotiated commercial licenses.

If your contribution includes patent rights that would necessarily be infringed by using that contribution, you also grant the project and downstream users the patent permission necessary to use the contribution as incorporated into the project.

By submitting a contribution, you are saying that you have the authority to grant those rights.

This permission exists so that accepting a contribution does not later break the relationship between the community version of the project and its commercial licensing model.

If you do not want to grant those rights, do not submit copyrightable material for inclusion.

You can still participate through discussion, testing, bug reports, questions, observations, research, and other forms of contribution that do not require that grant.

## ACCEPTANCE

Contribution is a relationship, not an automatic claim on the project.

Submitting something does not guarantee that it will be merged.

A contribution may be declined because it conflicts with the architecture, creates too much maintenance cost, duplicates existing work, introduces licensing or provenance problems, arrives at the wrong time, changes an important relationship in a way the project does not want to preserve, or simply belongs somewhere else.

That does not mean the contribution was worthless.

It does not mean the contributor was wrong to offer it.

Community requires both the freedom to contribute and the freedom to decide what becomes part of the shared system.

Meta Apollo is interested in **relationships that produce growth without destroying continuity**.

That is the relationship we are trying to build here.
